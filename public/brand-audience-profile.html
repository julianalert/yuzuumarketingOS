01What the brand is

Positioning

Done-for-you digital product build for Instagram creators, on revenue share. We build the product your audience already asked for, you keep 70%, it costs you nothing.

Yuzuu doesn't sell software and it doesn't sell a course platform. It sells the removal of the build. The competitor isn't Kajabi or Gumroad, it's the unfinished course sitting in Notion since spring and the guilt attached to it.

The promise on the page is unusually precise: twenty minutes of creator time turns an audience into a shippable $27 personalized product with quiz, sales page, checkout and delivery. Precision is doing the persuasion work here, not enthusiasm.

Category frame

Not a tool
No blank page, no login, no learning curve. That's positioned as the failure mode of course platforms.
Not an agency
No quote, no scoping call, no invoice. Rev share only, so there's nothing to approve internally.
Not a ghostwriter
The output is a system that holds across a thousand buyers, not one document.

Risk reversal is the strongest asset in the whole offer and should lead every conversation: no build cost, no monthly fee, no minimum term, nothing goes live without three approved samples, buyer list exportable at any time, creator keeps the build if they leave.

The six proof mechanisms

All six exist to answer one objection: couldn't I just do this with ChatGPT? The answer positioned is quality control at scale, not generation.

Deliberate constraints

  • One product per creator. One that sells beats three that half-work.
  • No art direction. Every page looks the same on purpose.
  • No software, tutorials, or physical products.

03Voice and brand codes

Flat, unhyped, slightly self-deprecating, anti-guru. Short sentences. It names the creator's failures without insulting them, and it says no out loud. It reads like a competent friend, not a landing page.

DoesKeep
States prices and percentages plainly. Admits what doesn't work yet. Uses the creator's own excuses back at them.
Doesn'tAvoid
Promise income. Use "transform", "unlock", "10x". Show fake dashboards or revenue screenshots.

Codes

  • Lowercase wordmark, one page template for everyone, by design.
  • $27 anchor, 70/30 split, "twenty minutes" as the time promise.
  • The Maya-and-Tom device: same product, same price, two visibly different documents. This is the single most persuasive block on the site and should be reused in every deck, DM and email.

04The real qualifier

Screening rule

Follower count is the wrong filter. The site says 10k–1M, but the actual predictor of a good build is question density: how many commenters describe their own situation and ask what to do about it.

Two-minute screen before any outreach

Count first-person problemsAcross the last 12 posts, count comments containing a real situation ("I wake at 3am and…"). Good: 15+ per post, recurring themes. Bad: emoji, tags, "where's the top from".
Test the 30-day shapeDoes the topic map to "here's where you are, here's your next 30 days"? Sleep, habits, training, language, money, plants, focus, confidence, running, budgeting, postpartum, ADHD systems.
Check for a shelfLink in bio with nothing to buy, or a $12 PDF doing eight sales a month, is the strongest single buying signal there is.

01The Stalled Shipper

Core segment

Most keen to buy. Should take roughly 70% of the first cohort.

Size30k–150k followers
Age28–42, female-skewed
Income today$500–$4k / month
Close speedDays

Ex-practitioner or current one: nutritionist, sleep consultant, PT, coach, language teacher, ex-corporate finance person. Built the audience on genuinely useful carousels and reels over two to four years. Still has a day job or a client load.

The defining fact

They have already tried to build a digital product and failed to finish it. There's a half-built course, an outline, a Canva workbook at 60%, a domain bought in January. They know the audience wants it. They ran out of evenings. Everything about Yuzuu's offer is aimed at this exact person.

I know I should have something to sell, people keep asking me for a plan.
I started the course but I don't have time to record twenty videos.
I don't want to be salesy about it.

Why they buy fast

The $0 removes the objection they can articulate out loud. The twenty minutes removes the real one. Approving three samples is something they can do on a Sunday. It closes a loop that has been nagging them for a year.

Opening move

Hook
Screenshot three real comments from their last two posts, all describing the same problem.
Line
"These three people are describing the same problem. There's a $27 product in there and we'll build the whole thing free — quiz, page, checkout. You keep 70%."
Ask
Twenty minutes, split across two sittings. Nothing goes live unless they approve it.

Expected outcome

On an engaged 50k audience: roughly 40–150 sales in the first 60 days, $1,000–$4,000 gross, Yuzuu keeps $300–$1,200. Treat these as working assumptions to validate against the first cohort, not as figures to quote to a creator.

Where to find them

  • Niche hashtag clusters, and the comment sections of the bigger accounts in the same niche.
  • Link-in-bio pages (Stan, Beacons, Linktree) with a thin or empty product shelf.
  • Anyone who has posted "working on something big, coming soon" more than two months ago.

02The Full-Time Operator

Would buy, after diligence

Higher ceiling per build, slower close, more negotiation.

Size150k–1M followers
Age30–45
Income today$8k–$40k / month
Close speedWeeks

Full-time creator with an assistant or an editor. Runs the account as a business with a spreadsheet. Already sells: brand deals with a rate card, a course at $197–$997, maybe newsletter sponsorships.

Why they'd buy

Not because they can't build it. Because a low-ticket front-end product and an owned buyer list are things they've been meaning to add, and this arrives with zero build cost and zero ops. The $27 product feeds their existing higher-ticket offer. The exportable buyer list is the actual prize.

What's the path from the $27 to my main program?
Who owns the customer data?
Can I white-label the page?

Objections, in the order they arrive

ObjectionStatus
Brand controlHard blocker. "Every Yuzuu page looks the same on purpose" is a no for this persona. No good answer exists yet.

CannibalizationAnswerable. Position as a paid lead magnet that feeds the $497, not a competitor to it.
Platform riskAnswerable. List export, no lock-in, they keep the build.

Honest read

Recommendation

Poor fit for the first cohort. They negotiate, they want custom branding, and one churning takes real revenue with it. Take them inbound only. The current offer is optimized against them, which is a defensible choice — but a flat-fee or 90/10 tier is needed before this segment converts at scale.

Expected outcome

300–1,500 sales in 60 days, $8,000–$40,000 gross. This is the segment where 30% starts to feel large to both sides.

03The Credentialed Micro-Expert

Low revenue, high strategic value

Worth building at a loss, because these are the testimonials that defuse the AI objection for everyone else.

Size10k–40k followers
Engagement4–8%
Income from contentNear zero
Close speedSlow, then loyal

A dietitian, midwife, physio, CFP, occupational therapist or accredited language teacher. Posts as a professional, not as a personality. Income comes from clients, a clinic or a school, and the account is marketing for the practice. Extremely defensive of credibility because a licence is attached to it.

Why they'd buy

The audience is small but converts unusually well, because the trust is clinical rather than parasocial. Yuzuu's health, money and legal safety pass is the feature that makes this persona possible at all. Pitch it to them verbatim: no diagnoses, no guaranteed outcomes, no advice that should come from a professional.

I can't put my name on something that gives medical advice.
My professional body has rules about this.
My audience is probably too small to be worth it.

Objections, in the order they arrive

  • Liability and scope of practice. Roughly 80% of the resistance. Lead with the safety rails and the approval gate before anything else.
  • Audience size. Answer with the math: 20k at 6% engagement outsells 200k of passive reach.
  • Discomfort selling to their own community. Answer with the price. $27 is the number people pay without asking a partner.

Strategic value beyond revenue

A credentialed name saying "I read three samples and approved them, and they're sound" is the strongest available counter to the slop objection. That quote is worth more than the margin on their build.

Expected outcome

20–70 sales in 60 days, $540–$1,900 gross. Yuzuu keeps $160–$570, which likely doesn't cover compute on the first build. Accept that knowingly, for a limited number of them.

The third one is the quiet killer. A creator who approves the build and then never posts about it produces zero revenue on both sides, and nothing in the current process holds them to it.

06Two gaps in the current profile

Nothing addresses the fear of the audience's reaction

For the Stalled Shipper and the Micro-Expert, the deepest unspoken objection isn't quality. It's "will my followers think I've sold out, or that I used AI on them." The site never names it, so it never gets defused. It kills deals silently, in the gap between the demo and the reply that never comes.

The ICP is defined by capability, not identity

"It works when / not yet when" is a filter on what Yuzuu can build. A creator scanning the page still can't tell whether they are the person being spoken to. One line naming the Stalled Shipper explicitly — you've had 60k followers for two years and a course that's been almost done since spring — would move conversion more than any other copy change available on that page.
