01How the list was built

Skool's Discovery page is a client-side search over every public community. Rather than reading pages by hand, the underlying search index was queried directly with the free-only price filter applied, across roughly fifty persona-derived search terms — digital products, course creation, coaching business, health coach, personal brand, dietitian, yoga teacher business, monetise your audience, and so on — paging through results until new terms stopped returning new communities.

That produced 3,079 unique free communities. A keyword relevance filter (positive terms for creator/coach/expert language, negative terms for dropshipping, trading, crypto, agency-SMMA, cybersecurity and similar) plus a floor of 50 members cut that to 607. Sixty-six were shortlisted by hand, then each one's landing page was fetched to confirm price, admin count, activity and — critically — whether the community is genuinely free or a Skool shell fronting a paid programme.

Three were dropped at that last check

Own The Room, Creator Secrets 2.0 and Hero Academy all appear as "Free" in Discovery but state on their own landing pages that entry is paid or membership-gated. They are excluded.

Every community in this report was confirmed free at the price level, had activity within the last week, and has at least 50 members.

02The scoring model

The audience profile says the real qualifier is not follower count but question density — how many people describe their own situation and ask what to do about it. That can't be measured from outside a community, so the model scores the six things that can be measured or read off a landing page, weighted by how much each one moves the odds of a yes.

CriterionMaxWhat it measures, and why it's weighted that way
Persona match30How close the median member is to the Stalled Shipper: an ex-practitioner or current one, two-to-four years of useful content, still holding a day job or a client load. Weighted highest because the profile allocates ~70% of the first cohort to this one segment.
Product gap20Whether members lack a packaged digital product. A room full of people already reselling PLR templates scores low; a room of practitioners who only sell 1:1 scores high. This is Yuzuu's actual wedge — it sells the removal of the build, which is worthless to someone who has already built.
Audience asset15Whether members already have an Instagram or social audience with comments in it. Communities built around "no audience needed" score low — Yuzuu's pipeline needs evidence from real followers or it has nothing to write from.
Reach × purity15Member count on a log scale (4 × log₁₀, capped at 15), multiplied by a purity factor of 0.55–1.0. A 9,000-member beginner community is discounted hard; a 300-member room of dietitians is not. Log scale because the tenth conversation in a room is worth much less than the first.
Access & promo tolerance10Whether an offer can actually be made without removal — free entry, low gatekeeping, no stated no-self-promo rule, and not a hard funnel for a competing coach.
Activity & freshness10Last activity timestamp, admin count, and whether the community reads as live or as a dormant funnel shell.
Conflict risk−10 … 0Subtracted. How directly the owner sells the same outcome Yuzuu sells. A community whose founder teaches "launch your first digital product" is the best persona match on the list and the fastest way to get banned.
Read the score as a ceiling, not a promise

The score ranks how likely a room is to contain the person described in the profile. It cannot see inside the community. The two-minute screen — count first-person problems, test the 30-day shape, check for an empty shelf — still runs per creator, not per community.

03Tiers and allocation

Scores land between 62 and 87, which is a narrow band — no free Skool community is a perfect fit, and none of the shortlisted fifty is worthless. The tiers split on where effort is repaid.

Tier A — Beachhead (80+) · 15 communities
Join now, contribute for two weeks, then offer builds. These have the persona, the audience asset and the room to make an offer. If only ten communities get real attention, they are these.
Tier B — Priority (78–79.9) · 19 communities
Strong fit held back by one factor — smaller size, a competing owner, or an earlier-stage membership. Work these second, or first if a specific niche is wanted.
Tier C — Volume & proof (below 78) · 16 communities
Mostly the Credentialed Micro-Expert rooms plus the distribution plays. Low revenue per build, high testimonial value. The profile budgets ~20% of the cohort here deliberately.
Segment split of the fifty
33 Core (Stalled Shipper) · 16 Micro-Expert · 1 Distribution. That maps closely to the 70/20/10 cohort mix in the audience profile, with the Full-Time Operator rooms deliberately under-weighted — they are inbound-only.

08How to work it

Fifty communities is a list, not a plan. Joining all fifty in a week produces fifty notification feeds and zero builds. This is the sequence that fits a one-person operation and the 50-build cap.

Week 1 — Join Tier A only, post nothing that sells

Fifteen communities. Answer questions, share the two-minute screen as a free framework, be visibly useful. The profile's voice rules apply verbatim: flat, unhyped, no promised income, no revenue screenshots.

Week 2 — Run the screen inside the rooms, not on the feed

For each community, find members who post about an audience that keeps asking for a plan. Check their Instagram against the two-minute screen: 15+ first-person problems per post, a 30-day-shaped topic, and a thin or empty product shelf. Aim for ten qualified names per Tier A community.

Week 3 — Offer in DMs, one room at a time

Screenshot three real comments from their last two posts describing the same problem. Lead with the $0 and the twenty minutes, never with the 70/30. Ask for twenty minutes split across two sittings. Nothing goes live without their approval.

Week 4 — Add the Micro-Expert rooms for proof, not revenue

Therapist Marketing School, Dietitians in Women's Health, Health Coaches Accelerator, Escape Healthcare Burnout, The Collective. Lead with the safety pass — no diagnoses, no guaranteed outcomes — before anything about money. What you want from these is a credentialed quote, not margin.

Ongoing — one distribution play

Summiters exists so Skool community owners can find speakers. One summit slot puts Yuzuu in front of several of these rooms at once with the owner's endorsement attached, which no amount of DMing buys.

The measurement that matters

The profile names "% approved → posted" as the quiet killer. Track it per source community from the first build. If creators from one room approve and never post, stop working that room regardless of its score here.

10Caveats

  • Member counts and activity are as of 3 September 2026, read from Skool's own index. Communities on Skool churn quickly; re-check before committing a week to one.
  • "Free" means free to join at the price level and confirmed on the landing page. Several still gate their real content behind a paid tier once you are inside — that affects how much attention a post gets, not whether entry costs anything.
  • Persona match, product gap, audience asset, access and conflict are judgements read off descriptions and landing pages, not measurements. Reach and activity are measured. Treat the score as a ranked hypothesis.
  • Conflict risk is the criterion most likely to be wrong in either direction. Some owners will happily let a free done-for-you build into their room as member value; others will remove it on sight. That is only knowable by asking.
  • None of these scores substitute for the two-minute screen on the individual creator. A 62-scoring room can hold a perfect Stalled Shipper; an 87-scoring room is full of people who will never post about the product once it ships.
