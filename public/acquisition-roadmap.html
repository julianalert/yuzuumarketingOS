00The target, honestly

5–10 signups a day is 150–300 a month. The first round is capped at 50 builds. Those two numbers can't both be true, so decide now which one is real — because they imply completely different roadmaps.

The thing to fix first

"Signup" is the wrong unit. A typed handle costs a creator nothing and predicts nothing. The unit that pays you is a creator who is live and has posted about it at least twice. Everything below is measured against that.

What the funnel actually looks like

Working assumptions for cohort one. Nothing here is measured yet — that's the point of the first thirty days.

Twelve percent of handles become revenue. That number is the whole business. If it's really 12%, then 10 signups a day is 36 live creators a month and you burn the 50-build cap in six weeks. If it's 4%, you need 250 handles a day to matter and no amount of DMing gets you there.

So the target, restated

Days 1–30Proof
10 creators live, 3 with real sales, handle→live measured for the first time. Volume is irrelevant here.
Days 31–60Channel
1–3 handles a day from one repeatable channel. One case study with a public revenue number.
Days 61–90Volume
5–10 handles a day from email plus paid. This is when your original goal becomes the right goal.

You cannot skip to day 61. Not because of discipline — because paid acquisition on an unproven funnel converts at whatever the funnel converts at, and right now that number doesn't exist.

01Six things to fix before you spend a euro on acquisition

One week of work. Every one of these multiplies every channel below, so doing them after outreach starts means paying for traffic that leaks.

1. Wire live Stripe payments

Non-negotiable and it's the only true blocker. Until a stranger can pay $27, you're doing research, not acquisition.

2. Put a real product page in the hero

Build one complete public page at yuzuu.co/u/ — your own demo account, real quiz, real generated plan, real checkout. Link it from the hero as "here's one that's live." Zero-proof offers die on abstraction. One clickable thing beats every paragraph on that page.

3. Name PawCraft and Bloomia on the site

"A small team that built this exact funnel by hand, repeatedly" is the weakest sentence on yuzuu.co, because it's the one place you have hard proof and you're being coy about it. Name them, link them, give a real sales number. It converts the "who are you" objection into a demo.

4. Add the identity line

Your own audience doc calls this out and it's right. Somewhere near the hero:

Copy
You've had 60k followers for two years. There's a course in Notion that's been almost done since spring. This is for you.

5. Defuse the audience-reaction objection in public

The deepest unspoken fear is "will my followers think I sold out, or that I ran them through a robot." It's never named on the site, so it kills deals in silence. Add it to the FAQ, in your voice:

FAQ
Will my audience think I used AI on them? Some will ask. The answer that works is the true one: you wrote none of it and you read all of it, and it's built from what they told you in your own comments. That's why we make you read three full plans before anything goes live. If you wouldn't defend it out loud, don't approve it.

6. Ship the launch kit

The 40% who approve and never post are your largest single leak, and it's the same failure that left the course unfinished — a blank page. Fix it in the product, not with nagging. When the build completes, deliver alongside it:

  • Three story frames, ready to post, with the link sticker placement marked.
  • One caption in their own voice, drawn from their existing captions.
  • A 30-second reel script: the problem, the two-buyer contrast, the link.
  • Three replies to paste when someone comments "is this AI?"

Then a single email at day 3 if the link has zero clicks. Not a reminder — the reel script again, with "twenty seconds, film it on the way somewhere."

02The wedge: give away step two

The core move

Build yuzuu.co/read. Type a handle, get three product ideas with the real comments that prove each one, free, no account, no commitment. It's step 02 of your funnel, detached and given away as the first touch.

This is the single highest-leverage thing on this page. Reasons:

  • It removes the ask. "Let me build you a product for free" still sounds like a pitch. "Here's what your audience has been asking you for" is a gift, and it's one nobody else can make.
  • It is the product demo. The read is the Evidence mechanism, running live on their own account. You stop describing quality control and start showing it in the first thirty seconds.
  • It's the same asset in every channel. DM payload, cold email link, ad landing page, partnership deliverable. Build once.
  • It gives you a qualification signal for free. The read tells you the question density before you spend compute on a build. Accounts that fail the screen get an honest "there isn't a product in here yet" — which is on-brand and generates goodwill.

Cost control

Don't generate reads for cold lists in advance. Tease two real comment screenshots in the first touch — nearly free to produce — and generate the full read on-page when they click. That way compute follows intent instead of leading it. Budget roughly $0.30–$1 per read and cap daily generation.

What the read page must do

Show their comments back to them, verbatimSix to ten real ones, clustered by theme. This is the moment of "oh, they actually looked." Nothing else on the page matters as much.
Three ideas, each with a promise, a price, and the receiptsExactly what step 02 delivers today. Plus the wild card.
One button: build this one, freeWhich drops them straight into the existing pipeline. The read is the top of the funnel, not a separate product.

03Channel 01 — Instagram DM, by hand

Weeks 1–4. Doesn't scale. Not the point.

The first thirty days of outreach exist to buy you three things: the objections in the creator's actual words, the handle→live number, and three case studies. Volume would corrupt all three. Do these yourself, not with a VA.

Daily routine, about 90 minutes

Build the list to 200Run the two-minute screen from your audience doc: 15+ first-person problem comments per post, a 30-day-shaped topic, an empty or thin link-in-bio shelf. Stalled Shipper profile: 30k–150k, 2–4 years of useful carousels, a "coming soon" post older than two months.
Warm 15 accounts a day, three days aheadReal comments on their posts, from an account with a face and a bio. Cold DMs from an empty profile land in Requests and stay there. This step is why the DM gets opened.
Send 30–40 DMsEach with real screenshots from their own comments. If you're sending a template, stop — the personalization is the entire mechanism.
Follow up once, at day 4One message, no guilt, no "just bumping this."

The message

Message 1 · after 3 days of real engagement
Three people in your last two posts asked the same question about waking up at 3am. Screenshotted them. I build the answer to that as a $27 personalized product — quiz, sales page, checkout, the whole thing. Costs you nothing, you keep 70%. Want me to send the three product ideas your comments point to? Free either way, takes me an hour. If all three are bad you've lost nothing and you get to tell me they're bad.
Message 2 · on any reply
Here you go: yuzuu.co/read/@theirhandle Those are your comments, not mine. If one of them looks right, the button builds it and I cover everything. If none of them do, tell me which part is off and I'll go again.
Follow-up · day 4, no reply

Reply rate is high because the first message contains their own words. It collapses to 3–5% the moment you templatize it, which is exactly what happens when you hand this to a VA in week two. Don't.

Constraint

New accounts get restricted fast. Stay under 40 DMs a day, warm every account first, never paste identical text, and don't send links in the first message — that's why the DM above holds the link back to message two.

04Channel 02 — Cold email, the scale engine

Weeks 3–12. This is the channel that reaches 5–10 a day.

Creators in the 10k–1M range put a business email in their bio for brand deals. That inbox is checked, and it's checked by someone expecting an offer. Meanwhile every competitor is fighting over the DM. Email is the arbitrage here and almost nobody in the creator-tools space runs it properly.

Infrastructure, week 3

  • Three secondary domains (getyuzuu.co, yuzuubuilds.com, etc.) — never the root domain.
  • Three mailboxes each, SPF/DKIM/DMARC set, two weeks of warmup before the first real send.
  • Instantly or Smartlead for sending and rotation. Roughly $100/month.
  • Apify or Phantombuster to pull bios and emails from hashtag and follower lists. Roughly $50/month.
  • Cap at 40 sends per mailbox per day. Nine mailboxes = 360/day at full ramp.

The sequence

I turn that into a $27 personalized product — a six-question quiz, then a plan written around each buyer's answers. Sales page, checkout, delivery included. It costs you nothing and you keep 70%. So 400 well-targeted sends a day is the machine that produces 5–10 signups a day, and roughly 30 live creators a month. That's the whole answer to your question, and it's why the read page has to exist first — without it the click-to-handle rate is 5%, not 30%, and the numbers stop working.

List quality is the entire game

Segment by niche, one campaign each: sleep, postpartum, running, language, ADHD systems, budgeting, plants, strength training for women over 40. Niche-specific first lines beat generic ones by roughly double. Build 400–600 verified contacts per niche before you send to it.

05Channel 03 — Paid, from week 7

Only after the read→handle→live rates are measured. Not before.

Paid is the accelerator, not the discovery mechanism. What makes it work here is that you're advertising something free to people who already know they should have a product. The ad doesn't have to sell the offer, it has to sell curiosity about their own audience.

What you can afford

A live creator is worth roughly $300–$1,200 to Yuzuu over 60 days. At 12% handle→live, a handle is worth $35–$140. Keep cost per handle under $25 and you're comfortable at the bottom of that range. That implies a target cost per read of $5–8, which is very achievable for a free tool.

Meta · Instagram feed + storiesPrimary
Interest targeting on Stan Store, Kajabi, Beacons, ConvertKit, "content creator", plus lookalikes off your read-page traffic once you have 500 events. Creative: a screen recording of a read being generated on a real account.
The creative that worksTest first
Not a talking head. A silent screen capture: handle typed, comments appear, three ideas resolve. Caption: "This is what your comments have been asking you to make." Fifteen seconds.

Ramp

  • Week 7–8: $50/day, three creatives, one audience. Optimize for read completion, not signups — you need volume in the event to learn anything.
  • Week 9–10: $150/day if cost per read is under $8. Add lookalikes.
  • Week 11–12: $300/day if cost per live creator is under $150. Not before.

Budget roughly $6k across the six weeks. If you don't have that, skip paid entirely and put the same hours into doubling the email volume — email at 400/day reaches the goal without it, just three weeks later.

06Channel 04 — Aggregators and partnerships

Slow to start, then twenty creators from one conversation. Start the conversations in week 4.

Creator managers & small agencies
They manage 10–40 mid-tier creators and are judged on revenue per creator. Yuzuu is free money on their roster. Offer them a slice of your 30% — 10 points — for creators they bring and shepherd through approval. They solve your posting problem for you.
Link-in-bio tools
Stan, Beacons, Komi. Their weakest cohort is creators with an empty shelf, which is precisely your ICP. Pitch as a fill-the-shelf partner, not a competitor. Long sales cycle, start now.
Creator communities
Paid Slack and Discord groups for full-time creators, newsletter operator communities, niche coach groups. Don't pitch. Post one public teardown a week: a real read on a volunteer's account, results shown openly.
Course-platform refugees
People publicly complaining about Kajabi pricing or an unfinished course are pre-qualified and self-identified. Search "almost done" and "coming soon" plus niche terms across IG and Threads.

07The three loops that make this compound

Everything above is linear — more sends, more handles. These three are the only parts that get cheaper over time, and none of them work until you have live creators, which is why month one matters more than it looks.

Loop 1 — The buyer-side loop

Every product page carries "built with Yuzuu." A meaningful share of a creator's buyers are themselves small creators. One creator with 200 buyers is a few hundred qualified impressions at zero cost. Make that footer link to /read, not the homepage.

Loop 2 — Creator referral

Once a creator has money in, they will name two peers unprompted if you ask well. Don't build a portal. Ask in one line at the first payout: "who else should have one of these?" Pay for it properly — 85/15 on their own product for a year, per creator referred and shipped. It's cheaper than any CPA above.

Loop 3 — Build in public

Post the machine working, weekly, on X and Threads. Not milestones — the mechanism. A read generating live. A build that got declined and why. The swap test failing and rebuilding. Your quality-control story is unusually good content because almost nobody in AI products has one. This is also how you get the first agency conversation.

The week-11 decision

At 5–10 handles a day you hit 50 builds around week 12 and have to choose: raise the cap, or convert it into real scarcity with a waitlist. The waitlist is the better business answer — it lets you cherry-pick for question density, and "we're at capacity" is the most credible thing a free offer can say.

10When to stop and fix instead of pushing

Written down now, while it's cheap to agree with.

  • Posting rate under 30% at 15 live creators. Stop all outreach. The offer isn't the problem, the handoff is. Rebuild the launch kit and consider making a posting commitment part of the approval step.
  • Quiz → purchase under 1.5% across three creators. Stop acquiring. The sales page or the price is wrong and more traffic just spreads the failure across more relationships you can't get back.
  • Read → handle under 15%. The read isn't landing. Rewrite it before scaling sends — this rate is the multiplier on the entire email channel.
  • Two of the first ten creators publicly regret it. Pause and find out why. This offer is built entirely on borrowed reputation and that's the one asset you can't buy back.
If you only do three things

Ship /read. Ship the launch kit. Do the first thirty DMs yourself, by hand, this week. The email machine in section 04 is what gets you to ten a day, but it only works on top of numbers you don't have yet — and you get those from ten creators, not three hundred.
