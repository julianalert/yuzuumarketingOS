Acquisition — 4-week execution list

Twenty-eight days to one creator who is live and posting.

One goal, three plays running in parallel. No email infrastructure, no ads, no tooling you don't already have. At this scale, hand-made beats automated every single time.

What changed from the roadmap

The 90-day plan optimizes for volume. This one optimizes for one yes, which is a different problem. Three changes: don't build /read as a product yet — produce the reads by hand. Skip cold email entirely. And add the play that closes fastest at this scale: build the product before you ask.

The three plays, in order of how fast they close

PlayVolume neededClose rate
Warm intro15 asksHighest. Someone vouching for you erases the "who are you" objection you can't otherwise answer.
Spec build5 creatorsHigh. You send a finished product instead of a pitch. Costs real compute; worth it for the first one.
Hand DMs60–100Low per message, but it's the one that scales into month two and teaches you the objections.

Run all three from week one. Any of them can produce the yes and you don't get to pick which.

Week 1

Make it real, make it pickable

Nothing goes out until a stranger can pay and there is one finished thing to look at. You get one first impression per creator and there are not many creators worth having.

Day 1 — Monday~4h
Take Stripe live and buy your own product
Real card, real $27, real delivery email. Then refund it. Until this works end to end nothing else on this list matters.
Write down 15 people who might know an Instagram creator
Ex-colleagues from the fintech years, agency people, the BTP network, French startup contacts, anyone with a marketing job. You don't need them to know creators — you need them to know one person who does.
Send all 15 the intro ask today
Script below. Do not batch this into next week. It has the longest lag and the highest close rate, so it goes out first.
Warm intro ask · WhatsApp or DM, not email
Hey — I built something and I need one person to test it, not a hundred. It's for Instagram creators with 30k+ who keep getting asked the same question in their comments. I build them a paid product out of it — quiz, sales page, checkout, the lot — for free, and they keep 70%. I've already built two of these for myself, mypawcraft.com and getbloomia.com. Do you know anyone with a decent Instagram following in coaching, fitness, sleep, nutrition, language, money? Even loosely. I'd just want twenty minutes of their time and they'd own whatever I build.
Day 2 — Tuesday~5h
Ship one complete public demo product
A live yuzuu.co/u/ page under a demo handle. Working quiz, real generated plan, live checkout. This is the single most important asset you build all month — every message you send links to it.
Record a 40-second screen capture of it working
Quiz answered, plan appearing. Silent, no face. This is what you send when someone asks "what does it look like." Far better than the site.
Put PawCraft and Bloomia on the site by name
Replace "a small team that built this exact funnel." Name them, link them, add one real number. It's your only hard proof and it's currently hidden.
Add the identity line near the hero
"You've had 60k followers for two years and a course that's been almost done since spring. This is for you." One line, biggest conversion lever available on that page.
Day 3 — Wednesday~4h
Build the target list — 40 accounts, not 200
One spreadsheet: handle, niche, followers, count of first-person problem comments on the last 3 posts, what's in the link in bio, the best single comment you found. That last column is what you'll paste into the DM.
Screen hard on question density, not follower count
Keep: 15+ comments per post where someone describes their own situation, recurring theme, thin or empty product shelf. Cut: big accounts with emoji comments. A 25k account with real questions beats a 300k account that just gets watched.
Add 10 French creators to the list
Lower competition, nobody is pitching them this, and you can send a voice note in French — which converts far better than text from a stranger. Keep them separate; if the English list stalls in week 3 this is your pivot.
Day 4 — Thursday~3h
Fix your Instagram sending account
Real photo of you, bio naming what you do, link to yuzuu.co. Six posts before you DM anyone: the demo capture, a two-buyer comparison, a build that got declined and why. A DM from an empty profile lands in Requests and dies there.
Start warming: 10 accounts a day, real comments
Not "great post 🔥". Answer a question in their comments properly. Do this every day from here to day 28, no exceptions. It's the reason your DM gets opened three days later.
Set up the tracking sheet
One row per creator: first touch date, channel, replied y/n, read sent, objection raised in their words, outcome. The objection column is the most valuable thing you'll own at the end of this month.
Day 5 — Friday~3h
Pick the 5 spec-build targets
From the 40, choose the five with the loudest, most repetitive comment sections. Weight toward the credentialed micro-experts — dietitian, midwife, physio, sleep consultant. Small audience, clinical trust, and their testimonial is the one that defuses the AI objection for everyone after them.
Write the manual read for all five
Six to ten of their real comments quoted verbatim and clustered, then three product ideas with a promise, a price and the receipts. One page each. Do it by hand through your own pipeline — do not build the tool this month.
Chase any warm intro that went quiet
One nudge to the 15. About half will need it.
Weekend~6h
Run 5 complete speculative builds
Full product for each of the five, on a private preview link. Nothing published, nothing under their name in public — you're building the thing you'd hand them on day one anyway. Track the compute cost per build; you need that number for everything later.
Guardrail on spec builds

Private preview links only, no indexing, their name nowhere public until they say yes. If one declines, delete it and tell them you did. This play only works because it reads as generous — the moment it feels presumptuous it costs you the account and the story.

Week 2

Send the finished thing

Five people receive a completed product they didn't ask for. Everyone else gets a normal DM. Target for the week: five real conversations.

Day 8 — Monday~3h
Send spec build 1 and 2
One at a time, individually written, at 8–10am their local time. Watch what happens before sending the rest.
15 warm-up comments
Next batch of accounts, three days ahead of their DM.
Spec build message · IG DM, split into two sends
Marie — this is going to sound odd. I built you a product. It's finished. Forty-something people in your comments this month asked some version of "what do I actually do when I wake up at 3am." Three of them are in the screenshot. I built the answer as a $27 product: six questions, then a plan written around each person's answers. Not a PDF with their name at the top. Nothing is public and your name is nowhere. It's just sitting on a private link.
Second message, immediately after
Here it is: [link] Take the quiz yourself, as one of your followers. Ninety seconds. If it's good you can have it. Costs you nothing, ever, and you keep 70% of every sale. If it's not good, tell me which part is wrong — that's genuinely useful to me and I'll delete it.
Day 9 — Tuesday~3h
Send spec builds 3, 4 and 5
Adjust the wording based on how the first two landed.
Start hand DMs: 15 a day
To accounts you warmed last week. Real comment screenshots in message one, link held back to message two.
15 warm-up comments
Keep the pipeline three days ahead.
Standard DM · message 1
Three people in your last two posts asked the same question about [specific thing]. Screenshotted them. I turn that into a $27 personalized product — quiz, sales page, checkout, delivery. It costs you nothing to build and you keep 70%. Want me to send you the three product ideas your comments point to? Free either way. If all three are bad you get to tell me they're bad.
Message 2 · on any reply
Here they are: [read link] Those are your comments, not my guesses. And here's one that's actually live so you can see the shape of it: [demo link] — take the quiz, it takes ninety seconds. If one of the three looks right I'll build the whole thing this week and you approve three real samples before anything goes live.
Days 10–12 — Wed to Fri~3h/day
15 DMs + 15 warm comments, every day
Same rhythm. Never paste identical text — Instagram flags it and the personalization is the entire mechanism.
Follow up on day 4 of silence, once
One message. No "just bumping this."
Log every objection verbatim
Their exact words, not your summary. By day 28 this file is worth more than the case study.
Work every warm intro that landed
These come with a 20-minute call attached. Take it. Send the demo link before the call, never during.
The four answers to have ready

Will it sound like me? It's written from your captions and comments, and you read three full plans before anything goes live. Is it AI slop? We compare plans against each other and rebuild if they're not different enough, and some accounts get declined. 30% forever? No monthly fee, no term, list is yours, you keep the build if you leave. Will my audience think I used AI on them? Some will ask. You wrote none of it and read all of it, and it's built from what they told you in your own comments.

Week 3

Close one

By now you have replies and objections. This week is volume plus conversion, and one honest look at whether the message is the problem.

Day 15 — Monday~2h
Review the sheet properly before sending anything
Three numbers: reply rate, reply→read, read→yes. Then rewrite the DM based on the objection that came up most. Sending week 3 with week 2's message is the most common way this month fails.
Diagnose honestly
Under 10% replies means targeting or the first line. Replies but no reads means the ask is too big — lead with the demo link instead. Reads but no yes means it's trust, and only the case study fixes that, so push harder on warm intros.
Days 15–19~3h/day
20 DMs a day, revised message
Up from 15. Stay under 40 or the account gets restricted.
Open the French list with voice notes
A 30-second voice note in French from a real person is close to unfair against a text DM from a stranger. If English is converting badly, this becomes the main play.
Add the bio-email channel by hand
Many of your 40 list a business email. Send the same message there, individually, no tooling. It reaches the ones who never open Requests.
Run 3 more spec builds for the best non-repliers
If the spec play produced the strongest responses in week 2, that's your answer about where to spend the compute.
Post one build-in-public piece
The declined account and why, or the swap test failing. Costs 20 minutes, and it's what makes you look real when someone checks your profile before replying.
When someone says yes — the 48-hour runbook

Reply within the hour. Confirm the idea they picked in one message and nothing else. Build the same day. Samples in their inbox within 24 hours with one line: read these like one of your followers would. Rebuild same-day on any rejection. Then get the launch kit into their hands before they approve, not after — three story frames, a caption in their voice, a 30-second reel script, and three replies for when someone comments "is this AI?"

Week 4

Live, posting, and documented

A signed creator who never posts is worth exactly zero to both of you. Week 4 is mostly about the twelve inches between approval and the first story.

Days 22–24
Get them posting, and treat it as your job not theirs
Agree a date and time before they approve. Send the story frames the night before. Message them the morning of. This is the same person who never finished the course — a blank posting box is the exact thing that stopped them.
Watch the first 48 hours of sales live
Screenshot everything. First sale, first buyer email, quiz completion rate.
Answer their comments with them
Draft their replies to "is this AI" and "is this legit" in real time. First public reaction sets the tone for everything after.
Days 25–26
Get the quote while it's warm
Right after the first sales land: "I read three samples and approved them, and they're sound." That sentence from a credentialed person is the most valuable asset this whole month produces.
Ask for two names
One line at the first payout: who else should have one of these? Offer 85/15 for a year on their own product, per creator they refer and who ships.
Keep sending 20 DMs a day
The urge to stop outreach and babysit creator one is strong and wrong. Creator two is much easier now that you can name creator one.
Days 27–28
Write the numbers down
DMs sent, reply rate, reads sent, yes rate, compute per build, quiz→purchase, first-60-day gross. These are the numbers that make month two a plan instead of a guess.
Rewrite the site with the case study in it
Real name, real number, real screenshot. The homepage you have now is arguing. One case study lets it stop arguing.
Decide about /read and cold email
If the manual read converted well, that's your signal to build it as a product and start the email machine from the 90-day plan. If it didn't, fix the read before you automate it.

What counts as a win on day 28

OutcomeRead
1 creator live and postingTarget hit. Everything in the 90-day plan is now unlocked because you can name someone.
1 signed, not yet postedPartial. The offer works and the handoff doesn't. Fix the launch kit before adding volume.
Reads sent, no yesIt's trust, not the product. Stop cold outreach, spend month two entirely on warm intros and agencies.
No repliesTargeting or the first line. Rebuild the list around question density before you send another message.
The one thing not to skip

The daily 15 warm-up comments. It is the least interesting task on this list and the one that determines whether any of the DMs get read. Ninety minutes a week, every week.
